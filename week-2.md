# Nephite Records

### What blessings did the Nephites enjoy because these records were kept?

The records helped the Nephites remember where they came from and who they were. They preserved the words and commandments of the Lord for later generations. As Nephi explains in 1 Nephi 4:13–17, obtaining the plates mattered because they would help his people preserve the Lord’s commandments. The records also helped preserve their language and gave each generation a shared written source to learn from. Spoken language and memories can change gradually over time, but written records help keep important teachings and words consistent. As 2 Nephi 28:2 teaches, the things written in the book would be of great worth to those who received them.

### What blessings do you enjoy because the Nephites kept these records, and Joseph Smith brought them forth to our people?

One of the greatest blessings I receive from the Book of Mormon is guidance. When I read about what happened to people in its pages, I can liken their experiences and teachings to my own life. I can then seek inspiration from the Holy Ghost about how to face the struggles I am dealing with. I especially appreciate the gospel discourses Alma recorded. His teachings give me ideas and direction that I can apply in my own life.

# Creating Your Goal

### Five-year goal

Over the next five years, I will seek to develop charity by becoming more aware of opportunities to treat people with love and respect. I want it to be more natural to consider what someone may need or welcome, and to offer care in ways that honor their agency. I will pray daily for the Spirit to help me recognize these opportunities and remember to practice charity. I will regularly review Moroni 7:45–48 to remember what charity is, why I want to develop it, and what its effects can be.

### Discover

After hearing Elder David A. Bednar’s April 2026 general conference talk, [“All Who Have Endured Valiantly”](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/general-conference/2026/04/16bednar?lang=eng), I began working toward becoming possessed of charity. This talk has spoken deeply to me; I have listened to and read it multiple times. During spring semester, I chose that same goal for a religion class project called *Becoming Like Christ*. That experience taught me that prayer and scripture study help keep charity in my mind, and that looking for opportunities to act with love—especially by listening patiently—helps me put it into practice. It also reminded me that charitable care should respect another person’s agency and what they do or do not welcome. Based on that experience, I have adapted the practices for this goal. I will continue praying daily and regularly reviewing Moroni 7:45–48, while using one weekly reflection to keep a record of what I am learning. I hope this approach will help me follow through and keep working toward becoming more charitable.

### Plan

- Each day, I will pray for the Spirit to help me recognize opportunities to treat others with love and respect, and to remember to act with charity.
- I will read or ponder Moroni 7:45–48 and Elder Bednar’s talk regularly. I will use them to remember what charity is, why I want to develop it, and how it can shape my thoughts and actions.
- During the week, I will try to notice what another person may need or welcome. I can listen, offer kindness or practical help, or ask what would be helpful. I will leave the person free to accept, decline, or tell me what they prefer.
- Once a week, I will review one particular interaction. I will consider whether I recognized an opportunity to act charitably, what I did or could have done, whether I honored the other person’s agency, and how I felt about the interaction. I will briefly record what I learned.

### Act

I will begin again with daily prayer and regular scripture study, and look for opportunities to respond with care in ordinary interactions. I will keep the weekly review to one example so the record is sustainable. If I miss a practice, I will begin again without treating the missed day as failure. I understand charity as a gift I seek from God, not something I earn by completing a checklist.

### Reflect

In my weekly review, I will ask: What opportunity did I notice? What did I understand about what the person needed or welcomed? How did I respond, and did I honor their agency? How did I feel about the interaction? What might I try next time? Over the five years, these weekly reflections will give me a record of small changes and examples that help me see how I am progressing toward greater charity.

# Refining Your Personal Goal

### What are two or three steps you can take to improve your personal record keeping?

1. I will use voice dictation for my weekly reflection. Speaking my thoughts aloud helps me get my ideas out without stopping to edit every sentence as I go.
2. After dictating, I will organize my thoughts into a brief, dated entry. This lets me preserve what I learned and clarify how the experience connects to my goal.
3. I will keep the weekly entries together so I can look back over time and recognize small changes that might be hard to see from week to week.

# Personal Reflection Paragraph

Keeping a record of my goals and growth could give other people something to learn from and perhaps inspire them. I do not have posterity, but I would be glad if someone else could see what I worked on and find encouragement in it. 
This goal is about changing my nature, and that kind of change happens gradually. From one day or week to the next, progress may be hard to notice, but a record across months and years can make it visible. 
I can improve my goal making by being specific about what I want to accomplish and choosing something I genuinely want; even a clear, well-planned goal is difficult to pursue if I do not care about the outcome. Writing a goal also helps me align with God’s plan by turning a fluid idea into something clear that I can return to and act on. 
Speaking aloud helps me develop and express my thoughts, while writing lets me organize them, notice when ideas conflict or connections are unclear, and understand more fully what I want to do. Keeping the goal and my reflections in writing helps me remember my commitment and see how it is becoming part of the person I am striving to be.
